Part 1

Find your edge

Most agents look identical online. Your edge is the one clear reason a client should pick you over everyone else in your market. Answer these five questions, then write your sentence.

1. Who do you serve best?

Be specific: an area, a price band, a kind of client.

2. What do clients thank you for after closing?

Write their words, not yours.

3. What do you do that most agents in your market don’t?

Think about how you work, not only what you sell.

4. What can you prove?

Reviews, results, numbers, or a story you can tell.

5. What does a client miss out on if they pick someone else?

Say it plainly, without putting other agents down.

Your edge in one sentence

I help get by .

The test: could another agent in your market say the same sentence? If yes, go back to question 3. When it passes, put it in your bio, your ads and your first message to every lead.

Part 2

First-contact scripts

When a lead reaches out, your first job is to answer them and start a conversation. Fill in the highlighted words, then make it sound like you.

Reply fast
Say why you’re reaching out
Ask one easy question

The first text

Send it as soon as you can. Keep it short enough to answer with one thumb.

Hi name, it’s your name with brokerage. You asked about what they asked about. Happy to help. Are you hoping to move soon, or just getting a feel for the market?

The first call

Their request is your reason for calling, so lead with it.

Hi name, this is your name. You asked about what they asked about, so I wanted to reach out while it’s fresh. Do you have a minute for a quick question?

What got you looking at what they asked about?

If this all worked out the way you want, what would be different a year from now?

Asking for the appointment

Offer two times. It’s easier to pick one than to think of one.

The easiest next step is 15 minutes where I walk you through the thing they care about. Is day and time or day and time better for you?

When they say “I’m just looking”

That’s completely fine, and I won’t push. What’s one thing you’d want to know before you’d feel ready to make a move?

Only call or text people who gave you permission, and follow your state, brokerage and do-not-call rules.

Part 2, continued

If they don’t answer

One missed call doesn’t mean they’ve lost interest. Stay useful, stay polite, and keep going until they buy, sell or ask you to stop.

The voicemail

Under 20 seconds. Give them a reason to look for your text.

Hi name, it’s your name. You asked about what they asked about, and I have an answer for you. I’ll text the details to this number now. You can reach me at your number.

The email

Subject: Your question about what they asked about

Hi name,

Thanks for reaching out about what they asked about. Here’s the short answer: one or two helpful sentences.

So I can send you what’s actually useful: are you hoping to move soon, or just getting a feel for the market?

Part 3

What does a closing cost you?

If you can’t name this number, you can’t tell which leads are worth paying for. Fill in one column a month. The example uses made-up numbers to show the math. It’s not a benchmark or a promise.

Example Month 1 Month 2 Month 3
A. Ad spend$1,000
B. Leads40
C. Leads you spoke with20
D. Appointments4
E. Closings1
Cost per lead (A ÷ B)$25
Cost per appointment (A ÷ D)$250
Cost per closing (A ÷ E)$1,000
Call-to-close (E ÷ C)5%

Closings can land months after the lead came in, so look at several months together.

What your numbers are telling you

You’re reaching too few leads (C is low next to B): reply faster, and use the rhythm in Part 2.

Conversations aren’t becoming appointments (D is low next to C): practice the scripts in Part 2.

Appointments aren’t becoming clients (E is low next to D): go back to your edge in Part 1.
